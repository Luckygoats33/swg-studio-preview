Where does it hurt?

October 8, 2026 · Will Wiedenmann

A diagnostic question to ask before a redesign, a new ad account, or another tool.

A founder usually arrives with a request. A new website. Better ads. An AI workflow. The request is real. It is often not the first break.

SWG starts one step earlier. Where does it hurt?

The hurt is specific. Traffic that does not become a lead. A site that no longer matches the company. A team that forwards the same email because no system owns the next step. Paid media that buys attention the page cannot hold. A report that cannot say whether any of it worked.

Those are different repairs. Treating them as one marketing project is how the leak survives the invoice.

Diagnose, then align, then rebuild

Diagnose means gathering the evidence that can change the decision. The offer. The page. The inquiry record. The response time. The spend. The event that is supposed to count as a conversion.

Align means the people who touch that path agree on the owner and the next step. Marketing, sales, and delivery can each be competent and still pass the customer across a gap. The founder feels the gap because they are the one closing it.

Rebuild means the site, the campaign, the handoff, and the reading are connected on purpose. A new surface without those connections is a fresh coat on the same leak.

Three words as a test

Integrated. Synchronized. Resilient. On this site those words are a test, not a tagline.

Integrated: the parts share a record. Synchronized: the public claim and the follow-up match. Resilient: the path still works when the founder is not the router.

If a proposal cannot say which of those three it improves, it is not a system. It is a task.

The question is the offer

Can you fix it? With SWG, you can. The fix starts by naming the hurt, not by picking a visual style. The diagnostic on this site is a plain version of that intake. It maps a symptom to a scope. It is not a quote. The quote comes after a person looks at the evidence.

Bring the symptom you already have. You do not need the rest of the answers first.
