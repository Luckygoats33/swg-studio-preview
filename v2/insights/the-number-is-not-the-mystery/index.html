The number is not the mystery

October 24, 2026 · Will Wiedenmann

A price range is the easy part to publish. The scope it names is the part that decides whether the work holds.

People ask what it costs before they ask what it includes. That is reasonable. It is also how projects drift. A number without a boundary will be asked to absorb every adjacent hope.

The number is not the mystery. The scope is.

Three ranges, three boundaries

SWG Studio publishes starting ranges so a founder can tell whether a conversation is even in the right room.

Foundation, $12k-$20k, is a website that finally says what the business has become. The boundary is the public instrument: message, design, build, one next step, basic events. It is the right room when the site is the part that is behind.

Growth System, $25k-$45k, is not just a better site. It is a connected acquisition system. The boundary adds the path from attention to a record, the match between ad and page, and a reading the team will open. It is the right room when demand exists and leaks.

Interactive Flagship, $45k-$85k and up, is the site people remember and the system the team can run. The boundary adds interaction that explains the business, a system the team can extend, and the operating notes that keep the founder out of the middle. It is the wrong room for a company that has not agreed what it sells.

An optional Growth & Optimization retainer, about $3k-$8k a month, tends a system that already exists. It is not a substitute for the build, and it is not a cheaper way to skip the scope.

What the range is not

A range is not a quote. The agreement sets scope, timing, and fee before work begins. Timelines on the plans page are planning ranges for scheduling a conversation. They are not a promise.

A range is also not a score. Paying for Flagship does not buy a guaranteed return. It buys a larger boundary: more of the path, built so it can be run. Results, if we publish them later, will be client-approved numbers from a specific engagement. They will not be invented to decorate a sales page.

How to use this

If you know the symptom, start there. The diagnostic will name a tier. If you know the tier and not the symptom, read the scope and see whether your break is inside it. If the break is a company-wide operating system, a custom product, or a regulated clinical claim, that work sits with the parent firm, Support Wellness Global, and is scoped on its own.

Bring the boundary you actually need. We would rather narrow a range than discover, in month two, that the project was three projects sharing a budget.
